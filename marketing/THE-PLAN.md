## 1. The truth: you're not behind. You're on Day 2.

## 2. Your target is smaller than the fear. You need ONE job a day.

Here's the number that should let you sleep:

- You need **~$3,500/month** to live.
- Your average job is around **$275** (single items less, cleanouts a lot more).
- After dump fees + fuel + insurance, you keep roughly **$200 of every $275 job.**
- **$3,500 ÷ ~$200 ≈ 17 jobs a month ≈ 4 jobs a week.**

**Four jobs a week keeps the lights on. Five and you're building.** That's one job most days. Not ten. One.

Now look at the demand you're fishing in:
- Oakland County has **~500,000 households.**
- **Dozens** of estate sales every single weekend in your area.
- Apartment complexes turn over units **constantly** — Beztak alone runs 50+ communities.
- Thousands of homes sell, rent, and clear out every month.

You need to catch **17 of them.** That's not a moonshot — it's a rounding error of the stuff people throw away around you every week. **That's why this is impossible to fail if you stay in motion.** The demand is an ocean. You need a bucket.

## 3. The one shift that changes everything: your empty day is the weapon.

Right now you see "a full day and no jobs" as the problem. Flip it. **It's your single biggest advantage and it won't last.**

Six months from now you'll be doing jobs all day and you won't have time to hunt. **Today you have all day to hunt** — and your competition (busy one-truck guys) can't. While they're on a job, you're walking into the property-management office that will feed you cleanouts every month for years.

A full-time guy with a clean truck, a veteran story, and all day to show up in person is the most dangerous thing in this market. Use the empty days **before** they fill up. They are not downtime. They are your sales engine.

> **The failure mode is passivity. The success mode is 20–30 in-person touches a day.** Pick the second one, every day, for three weeks, and the phone starts ringing on its own.

## 4. The shape of your day (until the calendar fills)

This is what a "no-jobs" day looks like. Run this and you won't have no-jobs days for long. Samantha keeps the phone covered; you go make it ring.

**Morning — the B2B route (your highest-value 3 hours).**
Pick ONE city. Drive there. Walk into **6–8** of these in person — truck parked out front, shirt on, cards in hand, maybe a box of donuts for the front desk:
- Property-management offices (apartment + rental turnovers = monthly money)
- Real-estate offices (ask who handles estate/probate listings)
- Storage facilities (get on the abandoned-unit vendor list)
- Restoration companies (they need contents hauled after every water/fire job)

The CRM Prospects tab has the list and who to ask for. **In person beats email 10-to-1.** One yes here = recurring cleanouts for a year.

**Midday — convert + be visible.**
- Answer anything that came in **instantly** (under 5 minutes — this is the whole game).
- One Google post + one Facebook post (CRM → Channels → generator). Two minutes.
- Scan Facebook groups, Nextdoor, and Marketplace for "ISO junk removal / need this hauled" posts. **Reply first.** First responder wins.

**Afternoon — demand interception.**
Drive the routes where the work is already visible and leave a card or knock:
- Homes with a dumpster or a pod out front (they're decluttering NOW)
- "Just Sold" / "For Rent" signs (somebody has to empty it)
- Garage sales (offer to haul what didn't sell)

**Weekends — estate sales (the killer app).**
Every estate sale on its final day = a family that needs the leftovers gone before closing. Show up, hand a card, say *"I'll haul whatever doesn't sell and leave it broom-clean."* This alone can carry your first month.

**Every evening — 10 minutes.**
Log every lead and touch in the CRM. Send the review text for any job you did. Glance at tomorrow's Launch tab.

Do the math on that day: 6–8 B2B drops + a dozen interception touches + the group replies = **20–30 real human contacts a day.** Nobody doing that goes hungry.

## 5. How you convert (lead → booked job → review → more leads)

Getting the lead is half. Closing it is the other half. The rules:

1. **Speed is the whole game.** Most people call 2–3 haulers and book whoever answers first and feels solid. Answer in under 5 minutes and you win jobs on speed alone. This is Samantha's superpower on the phone.
2. **Quote fast, never blind.** Get photos → send a firm flat-rate price within minutes, not hours. "Firm price, no surprises" is your edge over the guys who won't commit.
3. **Price to BOOK, not to max — early on.** A $200 job that earns a 5-star review and a referral beats a $300 job you lost by being greedy. For the first ~20 jobs, your real product is **reviews and momentum**, not margin. Win the work.
4. **Always close with a specific time.** Don't quote and hope. "I've got tomorrow at 10 open — want me to lock it in?" Make it easy to say yes.
5. **Ask for the review before you pull out of the driveway. Every single job.** No exceptions. This is the flywheel — reviews are what make Google and strangers trust you, which makes the phone ring without you hunting.
6. **Follow up.** A quote with no answer in 24h gets one text: "Still want that gone? Same price, I've got Thursday." Half your "lost" quotes close on the follow-up.

**Chase cleanouts, accept single items.** A $500 estate or garage cleanout (from a B2B referral or an estate sale) is worth seven $75 couch pickups and comes with less driving. Bias your hunting toward cleanouts and recurring B2B; take the small stuff to fill gaps and rack up reviews.

## 6. The $300 — spend it like it's your last $300

- **First, if your truck isn't lettered yet — do that before anything.** Magnetic door signs or vinyl: *JUNK REMOVAL · Veteran Owned · 734-743-1877.* A lettered truck advertises 24/7 at every job, every B2B drop, every red light. It makes all the free hunting compound. Highest ROI money in this business.
- **Then put the rest into Thumbtack** to buy your first few jobs and reviews while the free engine warms up. Set a small weekly cap, answer in minutes, and report junk leads for refunds. (CRM → Channels has the win-setup.)
- **Do NOT touch Google Local Services Ads until you have 5 reviews.** Then it becomes your biggest tap. The free work above is what gets you those 5 reviews in week one or two.

$300 doesn't build the business. **The in-person hunting builds the business.** The $300 just buys a few jobs to prime the pump.

## 7. The honest runway (so you're not blindsided)

- **Weeks 1–2:** the build. Expect 1–4 jobs from your personal network, the first estate sale, first Marketplace/Thumbtack lead. This is the slow part. It's normal. Don't flinch.
- **Weeks 3–4:** reviews start landing, B2B drops start calling back, referrals trickle in. 3–5 jobs/week.
- **Month 2:** the in-person work from month 1 pays off. 4–6 jobs/week — **your $3,500 nut, hit.**
- **Month 3+:** B2B recurring accounts + reviews + LSA = predictable money that doesn't depend on you hunting every day.

**Two survival rules for the lean weeks:**
1. **Take almost any job early**, even cheap ones — each one is a review and a possible referral. Reviews now are worth more than dollars now.
2. **Tell every happy customer about the $25 referral offer.** Your first customers are your best salespeople.
